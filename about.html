About Caugia Studio

Caugia Studio helps executives and GTM leaders bring calm to complexity. We design systems that connect data, teams, and growth so that performance becomes visible, measurable, and repeatable.

Most organizations do not lack ambition or technology. What they often lack is coherence. As companies grow, their go-to-market systems fragment. Teams chase different goals, data lives in silos, and leadership loses sight of what drives results.

Caugia was created to bring order back into that reality. We help leadership teams design connected revenue systems that balance growth and efficiency. The goal is not to do more, but to make every part of the system work together.

When leaders can see the entire system, decisions become quiet and confident. Growth stops being reactive and turns into something designed.

Founder

Tom Meijer
Founder and Revenue Systems Architect

Tom has spent more than fifteen years building and leading go-to-market organizations across France and beyond. He began his career in sales, grew into leadership, and helped scale teams from two people to more than a hundred across the globe.

His experience covers every stage of commercial growth, from single-product start-ups to platform expansions and from one-market focus to global reach. At Contentsquare and Greenly he helped companies move from reactive selling to structured, data-driven performance, reporting directly to C-level leadership.

Having worked within both corporate and early-stage environments, Tom understands how structure, culture, and technology interact. Through Caugia Studio he now helps executives design GTM systems that scale with intent and operate with discipline.

Tom holds a Master 2 in Business Administration and has lived and worked in France for more than fifteen years.

Origin

The name Caugia comes from the original Latin word Caugiacum, the earliest name for Coye-la-Forêt, the French village where the studio was founded. More than twelve centuries ago, it described a clearing on the edge of the forest, a place where people paused before continuing their path. That idea still defines us today: progress begins with clarity before movement.
